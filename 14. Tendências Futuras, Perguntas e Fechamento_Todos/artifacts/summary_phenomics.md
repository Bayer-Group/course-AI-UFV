# Phenomics and AI crop growth models: state of the art and outlook (Oct 2023 – Oct 2026)

Phenotyping is moving from task-specific networks trained on hand-labelled images to general-purpose foundation models that need very few plant labels. In parallel, crop modelling is moving from either-or choices (process-based or machine learning) to differentiable hybrids that borrow the physics-plus-learning design proven in AI weather models. These two shifts meet in G×E prediction: dense, cheap phenotypes on one side, skilful forecasts and simulators of environments on the other.

## State of the art

### Seeing plants with foundation models

The turning point was the Segment Anything Model, trained on over 1 billion masks from 11 million images and able to segment zero-shot ([Kirillov et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICCV51070.2023.00371)). Within months, frozen general backbones (MAE, DINO, DINOv2) adapted with LoRA matched task-specific state of the art on leaf counting, instance segmentation and disease classification ([Chen et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1109/iccvw60793.2023.00067)). Label efficiency is now the headline result: a single annotated Arabidopsis image was enough to segment infected-plant time series at Dice 0.977 and transfer to a second pathogen at 0.966 ([Maciá et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13007-025-01432-2)), and SAM-CLIP pipelines reached 97.54% mIoU on thermal field images while generating releasable annotations ([Lin et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plaphe.2026.100264)). The next step is plant-native pretraining. SPROUT, a diffusion transformer trained on 2.6 million unlabeled field images, outperforms web-pretrained backbones on dense structural tasks; it is still a preprint ([Xiang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27519)). A caveat runs through this literature: generic models still trail fine-tuned specialists in some settings ([Chen et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1109/iccvw60793.2023.00067)), so the gain is mainly in annotation cost rather than peak accuracy.

### From 2D pixels to 3D plants, roots and robots

Foundation models are also changing 3D phenotyping. Depth Anything V2 combined with SAM 2 estimated plant height from one RGB image with R² = 0.948 across 350 plants ([Wang et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aiia.2026.08.006)). Feed-forward 3D foundation models cut smartphone-based reconstruction from 6.52 minutes to 1.58 seconds per plant (preprint; [Jia et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01753)). Self-supervised point-cloud learning matched supervised baselines with half the labels ([Xie et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plaphe.2025.100049)). Data collection itself is being automated. Autonomous under-canopy robots phenotyped nearly 200,000 plots across 142 fields over five years ([DeBruin et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-07890-7)), and root segmentation is being built into self-driving lab loops ([Sordo et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-63497-8)). Open benchmarks such as the Global Wheat Head Detection dataset, which grew to 275,187 labelled heads from 12 countries, made this progress measurable ([David et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.34133/2021/9846158); pre-2023 seminal work).

### Drones, generalisation and G×E

The persistent weakness of UAV trait models is transfer across years and sites. A wheat yield 1D-CNN scored R² = −2.9 in a new year, and fine-tuning on 20% of target data restored R² = 0.83 ([Kaushal et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/ppj2.70085)). Multimodal fusion of hyperspectral, LiDAR and weather data with attention reached R² 0.82–0.93 for maize plot yield, and the attention weights aligned with known growth stages ([Aviles et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2024.1408047)). On the genomic side, deep learning has long underperformed linear models. Two recent results suggest that adding structure changes this: graph networks over SNPs and weather embeddings beat a G×E competition winner on unseen genotypes and environments ([Morshedian 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013729)), and a structured interaction network reached 0.63 accuracy against 0.43 for BLUP (preprint; [Potze et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.09.05.674546)).

### Weather foundation models meet crop models

AI weather models are now operational-grade. GraphCast beat the leading deterministic system on 90% of 1,380 targets in under a minute ([Lam et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi2336)). GenCast beat the ECMWF ensemble on 97.2% of 1,320 targets ([Price et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08252-9)). Aurora generalised the foundation-model recipe to the Earth system ([Bodnar et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09005-y)). NeuralGCM showed that hybrid differentiable physics plus learning can run stable decadal climate simulations, although its authors caution that it does not extrapolate to substantially different climates ([Kochkov et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07744-y)). This research has already reached farmers: blended NeuralGCM/AIFS monsoon-onset forecasts went to 38 million Indian farmers in 2025 ([Google Research 2025](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/indian-farmers-monsoon-prediction/)), selected through decision-oriented benchmarks (preprint; [Masiwal et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03767)). Crop modelling is following the same route. Data-assimilated crop models stacked with ML cut wheat forecast MAPE from 7.79% to 1.74% ([Zhuang et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2024.109909)), and NeuralCrop applies the NeuralGCM design to a differentiable global crop model that performs better under drought (preprint; [Lin et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20177)). Digital twins ([Kim 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-45725-x)) and generative growth simulators conditioned on process-model biomass ([Drees et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13007-024-01205-3)) point toward virtual field trials.

## Outlook and lecture material

### Outlook and open gaps

Over 2026–2030 the likely workflow combines robots and drones, foundation models that turn raw images into traits with almost no labelling, and AI weather ensembles that drive differentiable crop models to screen genotypes in silico across thousands of simulated environments. Three gaps stand out. Most plant foundation models are still preprints without independent benchmarks. Cross-environment generalisation remains the main failure mode. Hybrid models inherit the out-of-distribution limits that NeuralGCM's authors acknowledge.

### Proposed slides

1. **SAM: segmentation without labels** — 1B masks/11M images; zero-shot competitive with supervised; one-shot plant segmentation at Dice 0.977. *Refs: Kirillov2023, Macia2025, Lin2026*

3. **3D plants in seconds** — single-image height R² 0.948; reconstruction from 6.52 min to 1.58 s; half the labels for point-cloud organs. *Refs: Wang2026, Jia2026, Xie2025*
4. **Robots and open benchmarks** — ~200,000 plots in 142 fields; GWHD 193,634 → 275,187 heads; roots in self-driving labs. *Refs: DeBruin2025, David2021, Sordo2024*
5. **Fixing the G×E generalisation gap** — transfer learning R² −2.9 → 0.83; GNN beats the competition winner; SINN 0.63 vs BLUP 0.43. *Refs: Kaushal2026, Morshedian2026, Potze2025*
6. **AI weather reaches the field** — GraphCast 90%/1,380 targets; GenCast 97.2%/1,320; 38M farmers received monsoon forecasts. *Refs: Lam2023, Price2025, Google2025, Masiwal2026*
7. **Hybrid crop models and digital twins** — MAPE 7.79% → 1.74%; NeuralCrop better under drought; mandarin twin at ~185,000 ha. *Refs: Zhuang2024, Lin2025, Kochkov2024, Kim2024, Drees2024*
