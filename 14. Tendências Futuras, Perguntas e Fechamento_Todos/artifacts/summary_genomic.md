# Genotype-to-phenotype AI and plant genomic foundation models: state of the art and outlook (Oct 2023 – Oct 2026)

## State of the art

### Genomic selection: an honest draw
Deep learning has not replaced GBLUP in genomic selection; on current evidence the two are complementary. The largest tuned head-to-head comparison, 14 plant-breeding datasets, found DL frequently ahead on smaller datasets, but neither method won consistently, and DL's advantage depended on careful hyperparameter tuning ([Montesinos-López A 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fgene.2025.1568705)). Architectures are still improving: the CNN plus self-attention model Cropformer reported up to 7.5% higher accuracy than the runner-up across more than 20 traits in five crops, and its attention weights pointed to candidate SNPs ([Wang H 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xplc.2024.101223)). The defensible message for students is that architecture choice matters less than data, tuning and evaluation design.

### G×E, enviromics and open data
The larger gains are in modelling the environment. In the public Genomes to Fields (G2F) competition, the winner paired an environment-focused ML model with a genetics model. Other top teams used quantitative genetics, DL, crop models and ensembles, and no single strategy dominated ([Washburn JD 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/genetics/iyae195)). Explainable ensembles such as EXGEP beat Bayesian ridge regression by 17.37–42.35% on 70,693 yield records from 3,793 hybrids, and SHAP values showed which genetic and environmental features drove the predictions ([Yu T 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbaf414)). The 2024 G2F release provides ~180,000 plots, 5,205 hybrids, 272 environments and 2,425 SNPs ([Chen Q 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-026-07629-5)). Perspectives now argue for synchronised phenomics–enviromics data collection with satellites, UAVs and AI-assisted envirotyping ([Li H 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73097-x)). Multimodal DL can fuse these sources at higher compute cost ([Montesinos-López OA 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/genetics/iyae161)). Public transcriptomes are a further input: 21,612 maize RNA-seq samples supported accurate phenotype classification, although transfer from maize to rice was poor ([Wu Z 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/nargab/lqae184)).

### Plant DNA and RNA language models
Foundation models trained on genomes, rather than on phenotypes, are the newest development. GPN learned gene structure and motifs from Brassicales genomes without labels and outperformed phyloP/phastCons for Arabidopsis variant effects ([Benegas G 2023](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2311219120)). Its alignment-based successor GPN-MSA pre-computed scores for ~9 billion human SNVs, a design that could be transferred to crops ([Benegas G 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-024-02511-w)). AgroNT, trained on 48 plant genomes, set state-of-the-art results on regulatory and expression tasks and scored more than 10 million cassava mutations in silico ([Mendoza-Revilla J 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-024-06465-2)). PlantCaduceus transferred from Arabidopsis to maize across 160 My of divergence, improving splice-donor prediction 1.45-fold and translation-initiation prediction 7.23-fold ([Zhai J 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2421738122)). PlantCAD2 (676M parameters, 65 genomes, 8,192-bp context) beat the 7B Evo 2 in 10 of 12 zero-shot tasks ([Zhai J 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xgen.2026.101329)), so plant-specific training data outperformed parameter count. For RNA, PlantRNA-FM (1,124 species) raised genic-region F1 from 0.639 to 0.974 ([Yu H 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-024-00946-z)).

### From sequence to expression and fitness
Sequence-to-expression models now support design as well as prediction. Interpretable CNNs exceeded 80% accuracy across four species and explained expression differences among 14 tomato genomes ([Peleke FF 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-47744-0)). DeepCBA added 3D chromatin contacts (PCC 0.929), and promoter edits of two maize genes confirmed its use for design ([Wang Z 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xplc.2024.100985)). In applied breeding, PlantCaduceus-based scoring of >1,900 beet genomes traced a century-long decline in genetic load ([Long 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/g3journal/jkag264)). Pan-genome graphs plus interpretable ML identified a 5,002-bp Copia insertion that SNP-only GWAS would miss ([Wang W 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xplc.2025.101626)). In single-cell data, scPlantLLM reached 0.91 zero-shot annotation accuracy ([Cao G 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/gpbjnl/qzaf024)), and the preprint scMaize integrates 385,675 maize cells ([Cheng Q 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.01.742180)).

## Outlook
Three trends are likely to shape the next five years. First, generalist models are now generative. Evo designed functional CRISPR systems ([Nguyen E 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ado9336)). Evo 2 (preprint) was trained on 9.3 trillion bases with a 1-million-token context ([Brixi G 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.02.18.638918)), and genome language models produced 16 viable bacteriophages ([King SH 2026](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aec2657)). Second, crop-specific pan-genome models are appearing, for example the preprint OneGenome-Rice (422 genomes, 1 Mb context) ([Qian B 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.04.21.719822)). Third, honest evaluation will decide what is adopted. Evo 2's sensitivity to plant substitutions is real but modest ([Mantegazza O 2026](https://doi.org/10.12688/openreseurope.24244.1)), and DL vs GBLUP is still unresolved ([Montesinos-López A 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fgene.2025.1568705)). The main open gap is linking foundation-model embeddings to field-level G×E prediction; no reference here yet demonstrates that end to end.

## Proposed slides
1. **DL vs GBLUP: the honest scoreboard.** (a) 14 datasets, no consistent winner. (b) DL helps on small data when it is tuned. (c) Cropformer: up to +7.5%. Refs: MontesinosLopez2025, Wang2025Cropformer.
2. **G2F: open G×E competitions.** (a) ~180k plots, 5,205 hybrids, 272 environments. (b) The winner combined environment ML with genetics. (c) EXGEP: +17–42% over Bayesian ridge. Refs: Washburn2025, Chen2026G2F, Yu2025EXGEP.
3. **Measuring the E: enviromics and multimodal fusion.** (a) Phenomics–enviromics crosstalk. (b) Multimodal DL fusion strategies. (c) 21,612 transcriptomes as predictors. Refs: Li2026PE, MontesinosLopez2024, Wu2024.
4. **Plant DNA language models.** (a) GPN beats phyloP. (b) AgroNT: 48 species, 10M cassava mutations scored. (c) PlantCaduceus: 7.23-fold gain from Arabidopsis to maize. Refs: Benegas2023GPN, MendozaRevilla2024AgroNT, Zhai2025PlantCaduceus.
5. **Specialist vs giant.** (a) PlantCAD2 beats Evo 2 in 10/12 tasks. (b) Maize chromatin AUPRC 0.587→0.711. (c) PlantRNA-FM F1 0.639→0.974. Refs: Zhai2026PlantCAD2, Brixi2025Evo2, Yu2024PlantRNAFM.
6. **From reading to writing genomes.** (a) Evo designs CRISPR systems. (b) 16 AI-designed viable phages. (c) DeepCBA-guided promoter editing in maize. Refs: Nguyen2024Evo, King2026Phage, Wang2024DeepCBA.
7. **Next frontier: pan-genome and single-cell models.** (a) OneGenome-Rice: 422 genomes, 1 Mb context. (b) Copia SV found with pan-genome + ML. (c) scMaize: 385,675 cells. Refs: Qian2026OGR, Wang2026Setaria, Cheng2026scMaize, Cao2025scPlantLLM.
